Why Tulsa Sewer Lines Fail

Two Tulsa realities team up against sewer lines: expansive clay soil that swells and shrinks with Oklahoma's wet-dry cycles, and mature trees whose roots hunt for water in older clay and cast-iron laterals. Joints separate, roots pour in, and the line you can't see slowly chokes. If your home was built before the 1980s and you've had more than one main-line clog, a camera inspection is the smartest money you'll spend — it turns a $8,000 guess into a known problem with a known price.
